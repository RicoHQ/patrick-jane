    The man on the couch

    He grew up conning carnival crowds, got rich pretending to talk to the dead, and lost everything because of it. Now he uses every one of those tricks to catch killers.

    Background

    Jane walks into a crime scene in a three-piece suit, ignores the tape and picks up the one thing nobody thought to look at. He doesn't carry a gun or a badge. He carries a teacup.

    Colleagues find him charming for about a week. After that they find him exhausting, reckless and almost always right. He knows this, and he enjoys it more than he should.

    Full history under Record, in twelve chapters. The killer who shaped it is in the Red John file.

    How it all began

    From a carnival tent to the Bureau couch. Each chapter opens for the full entry.

    Act one

    The performer

    1. IChildhoodRaised in the carnival

      Patrick grew up on the road with his father, Alex Jane, a carnival huckster who saw early that his son had a gift for reading people. Alex put the boy to work as a child prodigy psychic and coached him to work the crowd.

      It was there that Patrick learned the fundamentals he still uses: watch the hands, watch the eyes, say something vague and let the mark fill in the rest. He learned that most people want to be fooled, and that a confident lie is easier to believe than an awkward truth.

    2. IIAngelaThe carnival girl

      He met Angela Ruskin on the same circuit. She came from a carnival family too, knew every trick he did, and wasn't impressed by any of them. They married young, left the carnival together and had a daughter, Charlotte Anne.

      By every account, including his own, those were the only years he was happy. He wears his wedding ring to this day.

    3. IIIFamePsychic to the rich

      Jane took the carnival act to television and private clients. He held séances for grieving widows, "read" businessmen for a fee, and bought a large house in Malibu on the proceeds. He was charming, famous and completely fraudulent.

      Police departments began asking for his help with cases. He enjoyed the attention more than the work, and he enjoyed being right more than either.

    4. IVThe interviewOne sentence too many

      During a television interview he was asked about Red John, a serial killer the police had been chasing for years. Jane offered a psychic profile on air and went further than he needed to, describing the killer as a sad, lonely little man.

      He was showing off. He had no idea who he was talking about.

    Act two

    The loss

    1. VThat nightThe note on the door

      Jane came home that night to find a note taped to his bedroom door. Red John had written to him directly, objecting to being insulted in public, in a polite and almost friendly tone.

      Behind the door, Angela and Charlotte were dead. On the wall above them was a smiling face drawn in their blood. Jane kept the house. For years he slept on a mattress in that room, under the face on the wall.

    2. VIAfterwardsThe sanitarium

      Jane broke down completely. He spent months in a psychiatric facility under the care of Dr. Sophie Miller, barely speaking at first and then speaking only about one thing.

      He came out with two decisions made. He would never pretend to be psychic again. And he would find the man who killed his family.

    3. VIIThe confessionAdmitting the act

      He publicly admitted that his career had been a fraud. There are no psychics, he said. There is only reading people. The admission cost him most of his money and all of his clients.

      It also gave him the one thing he wanted: a reason for the police to take his skills seriously.

    Act three

    The hunt

    1. VIIISacramentoJoining the CBI

      The California Bureau of Investigation had jurisdiction over Red John. Jane offered his services as an unpaid consultant and was assigned to Senior Agent Teresa Lisbon's team, alongside Kimball Cho, Wayne Rigsby and Grace Van Pelt.

      Officially he helped close ordinary murders, and he closed a great many of them. Unofficially every case was a way to stay close to the Red John file.

    2. IXTen yearsThe game

      For roughly a decade Red John and Jane played against each other. Red John left messages, killed people close to the investigation and planted followers inside law enforcement. Jane set traps, faked a breakdown and once shot a man in a crowded mall.

      The full account is in the Red John file below.

    3. XThe endFace to face

      Jane finally identified Red John as Thomas McAllister, sheriff of Napa County. He chased him into a park and killed him with his own hands, keeping the promise he had made in the sanitarium.

      Then, for the first time in years, he had nothing left to do.

    Act four

    After

    1. XIExileSouth America

      A killing is still a killing. Jane fled the country and spent about two years living quietly on an island in South America, writing letters to Lisbon.

      He came back on his own terms: the FBI agreed to grant him immunity if he would consult for them.

    2. XIIAustinA new life

      He joined the FBI in Austin, Texas, and asked for Lisbon to work with him. He traded the motel room for an Airstream trailer and slowly started living like a man with a future.

      He and Teresa Lisbon married. Last anyone heard, they were expecting a child.

    Red JohnClassified · Serial homicide

    The man who smiled

    A serial killer who signed his work, recruited police officers and spent ten years playing a private game with one man.

    Signature

    The smiling face

    A crude smiley face drawn in the victim's blood on a nearby wall. He left it on purpose, so everyone would know whose work it was.

    Method

    Patient and cruel

    Mostly women. Planned for weeks, staged carefully, and always with an audience in mind. He liked being written about.

    Network

    The Blake Association

    A secret network of police officers, agents and officials loyal to him. Members identified each other with lines from William Blake's "The Tyger."

    Interest in Jane

    An audience of one

    Red John could have killed Jane at any time. He chose not to. He saw Jane as the only person clever enough to appreciate him, and the game mattered more than the win.

    The vow

    Jane never wanted Red John arrested. He promised himself, and told anyone who asked, that he would find the man personally and kill him.
    Ten years later, he did.

    Senior Special Agent · Team lead

    Teresa Lisbon

    The agent who runs the unit, signs off on Jane's ideas after arguing against them, and is the one person he trusts without conditions.

    Background

    Lost her mother to a drunk driver when she was twelve. Her father started drinking after that, and Teresa raised her three younger brothers more or less on her own. She went into law enforcement and worked her way up to running the CBI's Serious Crimes Unit.

    Habits

    • Wears a small gold cross every day
    • Says "Jane" in one tone for every situation
    • Carries her sidearm and uses it when she has to
    • Defends him to the brass, then yells at him in private

    On record

    • Led the unit through the entire Red John investigation
    • Followed Jane to the FBI in Austin after the CBI was shut down
    • Nearly left for Washington, D.C. with FBI agent Marcus Pike, until Jane stopped her
    • Married Patrick Jane. Expecting their first child

    Kimball Cho

    Stone-faced, economical with words, and the best interrogator in the building. Nothing Jane does surprises him, which Jane finds restful.

    Background

    Ran with a street gang as a teenager before turning his life around and joining the U.S. Army. Brought the discipline with him to the CBI, where he became the team's steadiest hand in an interview room and in a doorway.

    • Almost never smiles. Witnesses report it is unsettling when he does
    • Always takes the hard door on a raid
